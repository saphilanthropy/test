> Core costs

# Restricted funding doesn't protect the mission.

It just moves the cost somewhere nobody is looking.

---

## The promise

Funders restrict grants so money lands where they intended it to land.

On its face, that's good stewardship.

---

## What actually happens

Core costs don't vanish when a funder declines to pay for them.

They move.

---

## Where they move to

- The unrestricted pot, which shrinks
- The finance lead working unpaid weekends
- A reserves line quietly drawn down
- The next grant application, dressed differently

---

## The uncomfortable arithmetic

A charity running at 8% overhead isn't lean.

It's usually one that has learned to hide the other 9% in programme budgets, because that's what gets funded.

---

## What good funders do differently

They ask what the work costs.

Then they pay a fair share of that — including the part that keeps the lights on.

---

## The ask

If you fund, ask your grantees what their true cost rate is.

If you fundraise, know the number before someone else sets it for you.

---

# What's the overhead number you've stopped arguing about?

I'd rather hear the real one.
